\begin{center}
  \textbf{\fontsize{12}{14.4}\selectfont Abstract}
\end{center}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\begin{singlespace}
\fontsize{11}{13.2}\selectfont
Ethereum Layer-1 provides strong security and decentralization but remains constrained by limited throughput. ZK-Rollups offer a Layer-2 scaling solution by executing transactions off-chain and submitting cryptographic validity proofs to Layer-1. However, practical performance of a research prototype depends on complex interactions between batching, sequencing policies, proof generation overhead, and data availability strategies. This report presents a modular ZK-Rollup benchmarking prototype designed to isolate and evaluate these performance factors under controlled synthetic workloads. We explore the design and implementation of the sequencer, executor, submitter, and smart contracts. Through rigorous benchmarking, we analyze the throughput--latency--cost trade-offs across different batching and data availability configurations. The results show that larger batches improve gas amortization but increase finality latency, while the TimeBoost scheduling policy improves fairness under burst-load conditions. EIP-4844 blob-based data availability reduces data publication cost by approximately 70\% compared with calldata, though RISC0 proving exhibits step-function latency at segment boundaries.
\end{singlespace}
\vspace{1em}

\noindent \textbf{Keywords:} ZK-Rollups, Blockchain Scalability, Data Availability, Zero-Knowledge Proofs, Performance Benchmarking.
