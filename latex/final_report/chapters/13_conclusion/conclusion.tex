\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This report demonstrated the design, implementation, and evaluation of a modular ZK-Rollup benchmarking prototype, aimed at addressing the critical scalability and economics challenges inherent in Ethereum Layer-1. Unlike production-grade rollups—which prioritize EVM equivalence and reliability at the cost of experimental flexibility—this prototype was explicitly architected to isolate and evaluate distinct performance bottlenecks under controlled, synthetic conditions.
By cleanly separating the sequencing, execution, proving, and submission stages into Rust microservices and providing a Python data-tools pipeline, the framework allowed for a systematic analysis of the throughput--latency--cost trade-offs in Layer-2 scaling. The empirical observations and codebase validations yielded several key conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Batching Performance:} In benchmark runs, the adaptive batching policy dynamically balanced wait times, reducing mempool queue wait times from \textbf{990 ms} to \textbf{252 ms} (a \textbf{75\% reduction} in latency) under low load, while matching the gas efficiency of fixed batching under high load.
    \item \textbf{Nonce-Aware Priority Scheduling:} The prototype's scheduler groups transactions by sender and sorts them by nonce before applying priority heuristics. In the benchmark runs, both \texttt{FeePriority} and \texttt{TimeBoost} policies achieved 100\% transaction execution success (0 empty batches), yielding FCFS-equivalent regular gas costs (~20.2k gas/tx).
    \item \textbf{Data Availability Economics:} EIP-4844 Blob DA mode achieved a \textbf{70\% cost reduction} over Calldata DA (\$0.057 vs \$0.188 per tx), while Off-chain DA achieved an \textbf{82.5\% reduction} (\$0.033 per tx).
    \item \textbf{Prover Economics and Segment Boundaries:} Swapping the mock prover with the real RISC0 prover backend validated the computational proving bottlenecks. Proving wall-clock time was modeled via linear regression as $T(N) = 75\text{s} + 5.7\text{s} \times N$. This high fixed cost is amortized effectively over larger batches, reducing per-transaction proving time. Prover execution scales in a step-function governed by segment boundaries (each segment representing $2^{20}$ cycles), adding ~140-150s of proving time per boundary. Regardless of batch size, the Groth16 proof size remained constant at exactly 256 bytes.
\end{enumerate}
Ultimately, the prototype successfully serves its purpose as a reproducible research framework, validating that scalability optimizations cannot be achieved in isolation. Enhancing one dimension (e.g., cost via EIP-4844 blobs or larger batches) inevitably impacts others (e.g., finality latency or prover computational overhead). The successful validation of the nonce-aware scheduler and adaptive batching policy demonstrates a clear path for designing efficient, secure, and responsive ZK-Rollups.
