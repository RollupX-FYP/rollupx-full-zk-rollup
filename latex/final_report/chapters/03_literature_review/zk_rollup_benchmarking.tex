\section{Rollup Benchmarking and Public Datasets}
\label{sec:rollup_benchmarking}

Benchmarking ZK-rollups is multifaceted due to complex deployments and differing design choices. Chaliasos et al. \citet{chaliasos2024analyzing} emphasize that creating standardized benchmarks for ZK-rollups is challenging because varying levels of ZK-EVM equivalence make common payloads hard to define. They provide analytical cost models demonstrating that per-transaction cost is a function of fixed batch settlement costs divided by batch size, plus marginal execution and data costs.

Additionally, recent studies analyze public datasets from deployed systems. For example, Silva et al. \citet{silva2025zksyncdataset} provide comprehensive analyses of zkSync Era traces. While these studies offer valuable, empirical insight into real-world rollup behavior, they are less suited for isolating the causal impact of individual configuration choices. In a production environment, it is practically impossible for a researcher to independently modify the sequencer's internal batching rules, scheduling algorithms, or DA modes under strictly identical, controlled workloads. Thus, a gap remains for a simplified, controlled benchmarking prototype that trades full EVM capabilities for the ability to strictly isolate and measure specific variables, such as how parameterizing prover delays impacts system-wide queueing.
