\section{Queueing Latency (Soft Finality)}
\label{sec:latency_results}
The Stage 1 experiments established a clear, mathematically predictable relationship between batch size, arrival rate, and queueing latency (the duration a transaction resides in the mempool before being sealed).
Under a uniform transaction arrival rate $\lambda$ (TPS), the time required to accumulate a batch of size $B_{\text{max}}$ is $B_{\text{max}}/\lambda$. The average wait time $W_q$ of transactions in a sealed batch is half the sealing interval, governed by the interaction of the timeout limit $T_{\text{timeout}}$ and the batch size cap:
\begin{equation}
W_q \approx \frac{1}{2} \min\left(T_{\text{timeout}}, \frac{B_{\text{max}}}{\lambda}\right)
\end{equation}
Evaluating this model against the experimental results at a steady arrival rate $\lambda \approx 18.5$ TPS (achieved under 25 offered TPS due to sequential gRPC client bottlenecks) yields high predictive accuracy:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Timeout 500 ms (s1\_to\_00500):} Theoretical wait is $250.0$ ms. The measured average queue wait was \textbf{256.4 ms}.
    \item \textbf{Timeout 1000 ms (s1\_to\_01000):} Theoretical wait is $500.0$ ms. The measured average queue wait was \textbf{516.3 ms}.
    \item \textbf{Timeout 2000 ms (s1\_to\_02000):} Theoretical wait is $1000.0$ ms. The measured average queue wait was \textbf{1017.2 ms}.
    \item \textbf{Timeout 5000 ms (s1\_to\_05000):} Theoretical wait is $2500.0$ ms. The measured average queue wait was \textbf{2487.0 ms}.
    \item \textbf{Timeout 10000 ms (s1\_to\_10000):} The batch fills to the size cap ($B_{\text{max}} = 100$) before the timeout triggers. The sealing interval is $100 / 18.5 \approx 5.4$ s, giving a theoretical wait of $2700.0$ ms. The measured average queue wait was \textbf{2937.1 ms}.
\end{itemize}
\subsection{Tail-Batch Latency Distortion}
A key methodological discovery in the benchmarking pipeline is the presence of severe tail-latency inflation due to boundary-effect distortion in finite test runs. For example, in the \texttt{s1\_bs\_0100} run, the reported average queue wait time was heavily skewed to \textbf{8,212.6 ms}, far exceeding the theoretical ~2,700 ms. 
An audit of the batch-level telemetry showed that batches 1 through 35 exhibited a normal wait time of ~2,700 ms. However, Batch 36 (the final batch containing 80 transactions) waited for \textbf{198,247 ms} (nearly 200 seconds) because the workload generator stopped, and the sequencer held the transactions in the mempool until the 200s timeout expired. Because the aggregator computes a simple arithmetic mean of the batch averages:
\begin{equation}
\text{Reported Mean} = \frac{35 \times 2,700\text{ ms} + 198,247\text{ ms}}{36} \approx 8,212\text{ ms}
\end{equation}
This tail outlier distorts the reported average. In practice, evaluating user experience must rely on the median ($P_{50}$) or apply an end-of-test mempool flush to prevent tail-latency inflation.
