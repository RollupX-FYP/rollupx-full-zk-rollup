This chapter presents the findings from the automated benchmark runs (Stage 1 through Stage 5). It is important to emphasize that these results are derived from a \textit{controlled benchmarking prototype} using synthetic workloads and parameterized delays. Therefore, the absolute numbers represent a configuration-level, relative comparison of trade-offs rather than a claim of production mainnet performance.

\subsection{Overview of Evaluation Stages}
The empirical results are structured across five distinct stages:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1: Batch Size, Cost, and Latency.} Evaluates the fundamental latency-cost trade-off using fixed batch sizes.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2: Adaptive Batching.} Attempts to evaluate dynamic batch scaling based on mempool load.
    \item \textbf{Stage 3: Sequencer Scheduling Policies.} Evaluates the effect of transaction ordering policies (like TimeBoost and FeePriority) on fairness and throughput.
    \item \textbf{Stage 4: Data Availability.} Analyzes the cost savings of Calldata, Blob, and Offchain DA modes.
    \item \textbf{Stage 5: RISC0 Proving.} Profiles the cycle behavior, step-functions, and fixed overheads of real cryptographic proof generation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Unsupported and Inconclusive Findings}
To maintain rigorous academic integrity, it is crucial to explicitly state which configurations or claims were \textit{not} validated by the experimental results:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Batching Not Validated:} As detailed in Section \ref{sec:throughput_results}, adaptive batching was not proven effective due to a hysteresis threshold trap.
    \item \textbf{Blob Fill Target Inactive:} Code audits confirmed that the \texttt{blob\_fill\_target} configuration parameter is inactive and unused by the sequencer's sealing logic.
    \item \textbf{BlobPacking Not Validated:} The BlobPacking heuristic triggered the same global nonce-reordering failures observed in other priority policies, resulting in failed execution.
    \item \textbf{Priority Scheduling Requires Redesign:} True priority scheduling cannot be effectively used without a nonce-aware grouping redesign.
    \item \textbf{Mock Metrics Are Not Production Metrics:} Mock prover and mock verifier results ($\sim$1,000 gas L1 cost) isolate queueing dynamics but do not reflect the computational bottlenecks or real gas costs ($\sim$250,000 gas) of a production zkEVM.
\end{itemize}
