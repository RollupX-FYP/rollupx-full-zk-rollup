\section{Effect of Batch Size on Latency and Cost (Stage 1 and 2)}
\label{sec:effect_of_batch_size}
Stage 1 and 2 sweeps evaluated the performance of Fixed and Adaptive Batching Policies under varying constraints of maximum batch size and load. Analysis of the raw data reveals the fundamental latency-cost trade-off inherent in rollup design: larger batch sizes reduce per-transaction L1 gas costs by amortizing fixed submission overhead, but exponentially increase mempool queuing delays.

For instance, configuring the sequencer with a fixed batch size of 25 resulted in a low average queue wait time of 1,392 ms, but incurred a relatively high gas cost of approximately 20,549 gas/tx. Conversely, increasing the batch size limit to 500 drastically reduced the amortized gas cost to roughly 19,459 gas/tx, but at the severe penalty of inflating the queue wait time to 74,209 ms. 

When evaluating the Adaptive policy under Low Load (5 TPS), the adaptive batching correctly prioritized latency by dropping to a smaller target size. This reduced the average wait time to \textbf{252 ms} (compared to 990 ms under fixed batching). However, because smaller batches fail to effectively amortize L1 verification overhead, the gas cost spiked to \textbf{55,882 gas/tx} (compared to 23,764 gas/tx for fixed). This mathematically demonstrates the limits of latency optimization on Ethereum L1 without transitioning to Blob or Off-chain DA.
