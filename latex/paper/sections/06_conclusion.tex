\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented the design and empirical characterization of a modular L2 ZK-rollup benchmarking prototype. Our evaluation reveals that modular data availability modes (EIP-4844 blobs and off-chain storage) dramatically reduce L1 transaction costs compared to calldata, provided that batch sizes are sufficiently large to dilute fixed L1 verification and posting overheads. Additionally, we demonstrate that a properly constrained adaptive batching policy eliminates the low-load queue latency bottleneck of fixed policies while preserving optimal gas amortization under high throughput. Finally, profiling off-chain proving reveals that zkVM execution scales as a segment-bounded step-function, establishing the validity proof generation pipeline as the primary constraint on L2-to-L1 hard finality.
