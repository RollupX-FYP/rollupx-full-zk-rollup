\begin{abstract}
The roadmap of Ethereum is based on the role of the Layer-2 systems as the main way to increase the scalability of the blockchain without sacrificing the security of the Layer-1 chain. ZK-rollups are one of the most attractive options thanks to the validity proofs that confirm the validity of the blockchain state transitions without having to re-execute the blockchain’s transactions. ZK-rollups are one of the most attractive options thanks to the validity proofs that confirm the validity of the blockchain state transitions without having to re-execute the blockchain’s transactions. However, the performance characteristics of these rollups depend on various design choices. Most of the existing rollup implementations are optimized for deployment, rather than for research into their architecture.

In this paper, we present a modular benchmarking prototype for zk-rollups, allowing various components of rollup architectures to be tested and compared independently. The goal of this framework is to study the impact of various configuration choices on rollup performance, which is normally a hidden aspect of rollup implementations.
\end{abstract}
